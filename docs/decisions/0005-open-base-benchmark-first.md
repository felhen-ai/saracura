# ADR 0005: Open base models, benchmark first

## Status

Accepted on 2026-10-02. Supersedes ADR 0004. ADR 0001 to 0003 describe the research runtime that preceded this
decision and remain historical records.

## Context

ADR 0004 committed the project to training its own checkpoint from a base model with a new, fully provenance-tracked
synthetic corpus, sealed evaluation capsules and fail-closed gates at every step. Between 2026-09-21 and 2026-10-02
that route produced 145 commits, 70 pull requests and no trained model: each experiment required a spec, a critic, an
executor, a reviewer and a release-grade pipeline, and the pipeline itself failed at a new joint on every run.

Meanwhile the open projects in this space (Laya, Kev, Von) had published complete fine-tuning recipes under Apache-2.0,
and a one-day research loop built on them produced measurable results: a Laya multilingual checkpoint fine-tuned on
real Brazilian Portuguese data went from 23.5% to 79.6% balanced accuracy on a 12-way marketplace task and transferred
to an unseen task (39% to 56% on Central Bank FAQ answer matching).

The scarce resource turned out not to be a model architecture or a harness. It was native Brazilian Portuguese
evaluation data with human labels and a permissive license, and training data for the decisions people actually make
with these models (email triage, ticket routing, lead qualification, moderation, agent supervision).

## Decision

1. **Saracura builds on open base models and publishes fine-tuned checkpoints under its own name.** The first checkpoint
   derives from `convaiinnovations/laya-multilingual` (Apache-2.0, 322M parameters); a larger one derives from the Kev
   recipe on `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B-Base`. Attribution is explicit in every model card. Saracura does not train from
   scratch and does not reimplement training loops that upstream projects already publish.
2. **The benchmark is the primary public artifact.** `felhen-ai/ptbr-typed-decisions-bench` collects typed-decision
   tasks over native Brazilian Portuguese text from permissively licensed sources with human labels, with train, dev
   and test splits. Every checkpoint, ours or not, is reported on it with balanced accuracy and a fixed protocol.
3. **Training data may include internal Felhen data; weights are published, rows are not.** Marketplace listings and
   internal documents enter training with labels produced by open-weight models; model cards declare their presence
   and the fact that they are not redistributed. No output of a hosted model with usage restrictions enters training.
4. **The method is a short research loop, not a release pipeline.** Experiments run as plain scripts in `research/`,
   measured on the same test sets, with the model artifact as the only progress metric. Release-grade rigor
   (provenance, sealed evaluation, calibration evidence) applies to what is published, when it is published.
5. **Generalization is bought with question variety.** Beyond the benchmark tasks, training includes questions of
   varied types generated by open-weight teachers over real texts and over synthetic texts of the target use cases.
   A held-out set of unseen questions and texts measures the effect.

## Consequences

- The README leads with the published benchmark and checkpoints, how to use them and the measured numbers.
- Issues #45 to #50 and #55 (the ADR 0004 roadmap) are closed as historical. The roadmap toward v1.0 lives in new,
  short issues: human-labeled evaluation of real use cases, calibration on real data, the 4B checkpoint and production
  use.
- The Phase 2 to 5 runtime, benchmark harnesses and candidate adapters under `src/` and `benchmarks/` remain in the
  repository as the typed API and historical research record; they are not the product and are not the critical path.
- Comparative claims stay bounded by the benchmark: a Saracura checkpoint is "better" only on the tasks, language and
  metric where the benchmark shows it, against the baselines reported alongside it.

## Reversal conditions

Revisit if the upstream base projects stop publishing weights or recipes under permissive licenses, or if measured
evidence shows that a from-scratch architecture is needed to reach a quality level the fine-tuned checkpoints cannot.
A difficult training run or a competing release is not sufficient.
